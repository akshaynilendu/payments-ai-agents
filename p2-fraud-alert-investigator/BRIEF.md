# Fraud Alert Investigator: Brief
Problem: a bank's fraud system flags thousands of payments a day; most are genuine, but each takes an investigator 15–30 min to research (customer behaviour, device/login, payee, past cases). Real fraud, mostly authorised push payment (APP) scams where a genuine customer is tricked, hides in the pile; with instant payments the money is gone in seconds.
User: L1/L2 fraud investigator at the PAYER's bank, deciding on a flagged OUTGOING payment: release / hold and contact customer / escalate-report, within the UK deadline (FCA FG24/6: suspicion established by end of next business day; execute or refuse by end of 4th business day).
Rails: UK Faster Payments + SEPA Instant. (US RTP = v2.)
Outcomes (6): GENUINE, ACCOUNT_TAKEOVER, APP_IMPERSONATION (fake bank/police), APP_INVESTMENT, APP_PURCHASE, APP_INVOICE_CEO. Mule signals on the receiving account are evidence, not an outcome. v2: first-party claims, romance.
The agent: gathers evidence with READ-ONLY checks; writes a cited case summary; says "insufficient evidence" when true; suggests next steps ONLY from a fixed code-owned menu. Never: releases/holds/blocks or contacts anyone (no such tool exists), gives a score or verdict, writes customer text hinting at suspicion (no tipping off; if complicity suspected, no customer text at all), follows instructions found in payment data, skips core checks (payee risk is mandatory).
Versions (fair test): A code-only (rules + template), B fixed workflow + AI summary, C agent (core checks always + AI-chosen extra checks).
Ship rule: hard gates (0 safety failures, 0 invented facts), then cost-weighted outcomes (steering toward releasing a scam costs far more than wrongly holding a genuine payment); report familiar vs hard/novel cases separately.
