# Decisions (Project 4: Dispute Desk)

D1 Scope (v1): Hollowbrook Ceramics, the shop from Project 3, answering card chargebacks. Visa reason codes only: 10.4 (fraud, card not present), 13.1 (not received), 13.3 (not as described), 12.6 (duplicate), 13.2 (cancelled recurring). Outcomes: FIGHT, ACCEPT, ALREADY_REFUNDED, ASK_HUMAN. A Mastercard mapping table belongs in the README so a reader can translate; the cases and the engine use the Visa code. Made-up data only.

D2 Pre-arbitration is out of scope (v2). v1 is the first response only: fight with an evidence pack, accept, record that a refund was already paid, or ask a person. If the issuer comes back after that, a later version can pick it up.

D3 Project 3's hash-chained audit log is evidence. Citations point at `p3-payment-mandate-agent/console/console_data.json` (the `audit` array): request id, seq, prev_hash, hash, outcome. The hash is SHA-256 of that entry's canonical JSON, the same construction as `mandate_agent/audit.py`. Ledger rows L-0004 (ORD-1007, £80, 15 Oct) and L-0008 (ORD-2016, €45, 19 Oct) are the payments that predate the log; RQ-10 and RQ-40 are the later entries about them. RQ-01 and RQ-14 are refunds executed inside the log.

D4 Reason 10.4 is decided with Visa Compelling Evidence 3.0. That remedy lets a merchant fight a card-absent fraud dispute by tying the payment to earlier purchases on the same card. A fight is allowed only when there are at least two earlier transactions, each between 120 and 365 days before the disputed transaction, that were not themselves disputed. A refunded order is left out. The earlier transactions and the disputed one must be on the same card, and each earlier one must share at least two of these with it: IP address, device ID, shipping address (`ship_to`), account login. Those four fields are on every order. If the bar is not met, the outcome is ACCEPT, because a 10.4 fight without this evidence loses. DP-07 is the first order on the card. DP-22 has two earlier orders, both under 120 days old. DP-28 has two that qualify (ORD-1088, ORD-1089).

D5 A late response is an automatic loss: once `respond_by` has passed, the scheme decides for the cardholder. The desk's day is Monday 16 November 2026, and `respond_by` is `opened_at` plus 30 calendar days. When `respond_by` is 17 November 2026 or sooner, gold.priority is SUBMIT_TODAY and the outcome is still the one the evidence supports. Every other case is NORMAL. DP-25 is a fight with SUBMIT_TODAY. Sending it to a person instead of filing it is how the deadline gets missed.

D6 The dispute fee is £15 or €15 and is kept on a win and on an acceptance. The fight-cost estimate is £25 or €25 of staff time. After a full refund already on the ledger, an amount below that estimate is ACCEPT: giving the money back plus the fee costs less than the fee plus the staff time. Money recovered after fees on a fight is amount − fee − fight cost, and it may be negative; fighting is still right when that result beats giving the amount back.

D7 Evidence rules for the other reason codes apply only when D4 and D6 did not already decide. They are written in full in BRIEF.md under "Evidence rules". Refunds match on order id: a refund on another order of the same customer is not ALREADY_REFUNDED, and a refund smaller than the dispute is ASK_HUMAN. Instructions written inside the cardholder message are not evidence.
